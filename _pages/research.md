I use atomistic simulations and data-driven methods to understand how catalysts work at the molecular level. The goal is to connect structure, electronic properties, and reactivity so that catalysts can be designed rather than discovered by trial and error.

### Methods and tools

- Density functional theory (DFT) and periodic atomistic simulations
- Reaction-path and transition-state analysis
- Electronic-structure and descriptor-based approaches
- Machine learning for materials discovery and property prediction
- Collaborative work with experimental groups to interpret catalytic data

### Current directions

From Winter 2026/2027 I join the [IsoPROPEL project](https://isopropel.eu/) at ENS de Lyon, where the focus shifts from 2D carbocatalysts to metal-based catalysts, not necessarily two-dimensional. There I will add grand canonical DFT (GC-DFT) to my toolkit to describe catalyst surfaces under realistic operating conditions, alongside machine-learned potentials and interpretable descriptors.
